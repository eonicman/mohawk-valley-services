HVAC Seasonal Guide for Mohawk Valley Homeowners

The Mohawk Valley sits between the Adirondacks and the Catskills, and that means serious temperature swings: sub-zero winters and 90°+ summer days. Whether you're heating an older Victorian in Amsterdam or cooling a ranch in Johnstown, this guide walks you through seasonal HVAC maintenance you can do yourself — and when it's time to call a local pro.

Furnace Prep for Mohawk Valley Winters

Our region averages 140+ inches of snow per year and temperatures that regularly dip below zero. A furnace failure in January is not just uncomfortable — it's dangerous. Here's how to prepare:

September–October: Pre-season checklist

  • Replace or clean the filter. A dirty filter forces your furnace to work harder and reduces airflow. Check it monthly during heating season. Standard 1-inch filters need replacing every 1–3 months; 4–5-inch media filters last 6–12 months.
  • Test the thermostat. Switch to heat mode and set it 5° above room temp. The furnace should kick on within a minute. If it doesn't, check the batteries and wiring before calling for service.
  • Clean the blower compartment. Vacuum dust from around the blower motor and check that the blower wheel spins freely.
  • Inspect the exhaust flue. Make sure the vent pipe from your furnace to the outside is clear of debris, bird nests, and snow. Blocked exhaust can cause carbon monoxide buildup.
  • Test your CO detectors. Replace batteries and verify the alarm works. Every home with a furnace needs working carbon monoxide detectors on every floor.
  • Schedule a professional tune-up if your furnace is over 5 years old. Annual inspections catch cracked heat exchangers and other safety issues early.
Local tip: Many Mohawk Valley HVAC companies like St. Andrews Heating & Cooling (Johnstown) and Efficient Heating and Cooling (Gloversville) offer fall tune-up specials in September and October. Booking early saves money and ensures you're ready before the first deep freeze.

During heating season:

  • Keep supply registers and return grilles unobstructed — furniture and rugs blocking airflow are a top cause of uneven heating.
  • If some rooms are cold, try partially closing registers in warmer rooms to redirect flow. Never close more than 25% of registers total, or you risk damaging the blower.
  • Change the filter on schedule — set a phone reminder. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of furnace breakdowns.

AC Maintenance for Summer

Summers in the Mohawk Valley bring stretches of 85–95° with high humidity. Whether you have central air or a window unit, maintenance keeps it running efficiently.

May–June: Cooling season prep

  • Outdoor unit cleanup: Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser (the outside unit). It needs at least 2 feet of clearance on all sides. Hose it down gently to remove dirt from the fins.
  • Straighten bent fins with a fin comb (available at hardware stores for under $10). Bent fins block airflow and reduce efficiency.
  • Check the condensate drain. A clogged drain causes water damage and can shut down your AC. Pour a cup of white vinegar down the drain line to prevent algae growth.
  • Clean or replace the filter. The same filter that serves your furnace serves your AC during cooling season. Dirty filters reduce cooling capacity by 15–20%.
  • Window AC units: Clean the filter (usually behind the front grille), check the drain hole isn't clogged, and make sure the unit is pitched slightly outward so condensation drains outside.
Safety note: Never attempt to add refrigerant (Freon) yourself. Handling refrigerants requires EPA certification. If your AC isn't cooling, low refrigerant means there's a leak that needs professional diagnosis.

Thermostat Tips That Save Money

Your thermostat is the single biggest lever for HVAC savings. Here's how to optimize it for our climate:

Winter Settings

  • Home: 68–70°F
  • Sleep/Away: 62–65°F
  • Each degree below 70° saves roughly 3% on heating

Summer Settings

  • Home: 74–78°F
  • Away: 82–85°F
  • Each degree above 74° saves roughly 3–5% on cooling

Programmable Schedule

  • Setback 7–10° for 8+ hour stretches
  • Morning warmup: start 30 min before waking
  • Don't turn the system off entirely — recovery costs more

Smart thermostat upgrade: If you still have a manual thermostat, a programmable or smart thermostat (ecobee, Nest, Honeywell) typically pays for itself within one heating season in the Mohawk Valley. National Grid and NYSEG both offer rebates for smart thermostat installations in our region.

When to Replace Your HVAC System

Most HVAC systems in the Mohawk Valley work harder than the national average due to our extreme temperature range. Here are the signs it's time to consider replacement:

  • Age: Furnaces over 15–20 years and AC units over 10–15 years are candidates for replacement, especially if repair costs are rising.
  • Rising energy bills: An aging system loses efficiency. If your heating or cooling bills are climbing despite consistent thermostat settings, the equipment may be wearing out.
  • Frequent repairs: If you've called for service more than twice in a season, or a single repair estimate exceeds $500–1,000, replacement often makes more financial sense.
  • Uneven heating or cooling: Some rooms too hot, others too cold. This can indicate duct issues, an undersized system, or a unit that's losing capacity.
  • Strange noises: Banging, rattling, or squealing from your furnace or AC often indicate worn components that may fail soon.
Efficiency standards: New York State requires all newly installed furnaces to be at least 80% AFUE (Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency), and modern units reach 95%+ AFUE. For central AC, look for SEER2 ratings of 15+ (the new federal minimum is 14 SEER2 for our region). NYSERDA and National Grid both offer rebates for high-efficiency HVAC equipment.

Seasonal Maintenance Calendar

Staying on top of HVAC maintenance prevents the most common (and expensive) breakdowns in our region:

Spring (April–May)

  • Schedule AC tune-up
  • Clean outdoor condenser unit
  • Replace air filter
  • Test cooling before first heat wave
  • Clear condensate drain

Summer (June–Aug)

  • Check filter monthly
  • Keep outdoor unit clear of weeds and debris
  • Monitor for unusual sounds or weak airflow
  • Close blinds on south-facing windows

Fall (Sept–Oct)

  • Schedule furnace tune-up
  • Replace air filter
  • Test heating system
  • Inspect exhaust flue
  • Test CO detectors

Winter (Nov–Mar)

  • Check filter monthly
  • Keep supply registers unblocked
  • Keep outdoor unit clear of snow
  • Watch for ice on outdoor unit
  • Monitor gas/propane levels
